# IMF Framework — Principles & Concepts

## Principles

1. **Prefer links over folders.** A note lives in one folder, but can link to — and appear on — many maps.
2. **Structure should help, not restrict.** MOCs are non-exclusive; nothing pins an idea to a single place.
3. **One idea per note.** Small, self-contained notes link together far more easily than long, mixed ones.
4. **File by concept, not by source.** The same idea from different books or articles should converge on one note, not scatter across several.
5. **Let notes accumulate.** A concept note is never "finished" — keep adding to it as you learn more.
6. **Build structure only when you need it.** Create a map once a topic has around 5+ notes and gets hard to hold in your head — not before.

## Concepts

**Atomic note** — one idea per note, self-contained enough to make sense on its own. One `.md` file, one claim, a descriptive title.

**Concept note** — an atomic note written in your own words and filed by *concept* rather than by source. The note type you'll create most.

**Source note** — a summary of one external source (book, article, video) in your own words, with a citation. Raw material for concept notes, not itself the knowledge layer.

**MOC (Map of Content)** — a note that's mostly links, curating and ordering the concept notes on one topic. It behaves like a tag and a folder at once — except non-exclusive: a note can sit on several MOCs simultaneously, and reordering one never touches the notes it points to. (The term predates Milo — Lion Kimbro used it in 2003 — but Milo gave it its modern linked-note form.)

**Home note** — the single entry point for the vault: links out to the main MOCs. Milo's original method also has a separate "Index" note sitting above the MOCs; this vault folds that into the Home note since the split doesn't earn its keep at a single-person scale.

**Link** — a `[[wikilink]]` between two notes: the strongest, most explicit relationship Obsidian has. Prefer it over tags for anything conceptual.
